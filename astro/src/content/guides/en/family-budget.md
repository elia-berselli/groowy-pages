A family budget does not require everyone to see everything. It starts with a short, clear conversation: which spending is shared, which period are we looking at, and which decision do we want to make easier?

## Draw the boundary before doing the maths

You can start with three groups: household costs, shared commitments and common goals. Personal spending stays personal until there is a clear reason to share it. That distinction keeps a budget from turning into mutual monitoring and makes the month-end review more useful.

## Use a short cycle

1. Agree a target amount and a realistic period.
2. Record only the spending inside the chosen boundary.
3. Review deviations with one question: is it an exception, a new habit or a category that needs to be discussed again?

In Groowy, Spaces help keep Personal, Family and Work contexts separate. Sharing is a contextual choice: before using it, clarify who participates, what will be read and which action may be taken on that data.

## Avoid false precision

A budget does not need to predict every cost. It should make the modifiable parts of a month visible: subscriptions, household essentials, transport, savings and unexpected events. Even a “miscellaneous” line can be useful when it is reviewed regularly instead of becoming a permanent drawer.
